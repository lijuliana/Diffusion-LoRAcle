### Summary

This is not a finished paper but a workshop-scoping/lab-notebook document proposing to port "LoRAcles" — a self-supervised weight-space reader originally built for text-LoRAs on Qwen3 — to image-diffusion-transformer (klein/FLUX-family) LoRA adapters, so that a language model can describe what a visual LoRA depicts by reading tokens derived from its weight matrices, without ever seeing a generated image. The document's own internal history is unusually candid: an original headline claim ("subspace projectors are the right feature for reading LoRA weights, beating canonicalised singular directions") is explicitly retracted mid-document as an artefact of evaluating on a "recipe-clamped" 25–32-organism gate set; once training-recipe variation is introduced, the proposed feature ties a random projection and the simplest baseline (top singular vector, u1). The de-risking work that survives is a negative result (per-direction SVD features are ill-conditioned because 59.2% of adjacent singular-value gaps are below 1e-2) and an adoption of the ancestor system's architecture, injection scheme, and hyperparameters wholesale. The stated headline going forward is the "reader" itself — an untested cross-modality, cross-architecture weight-to-language bridge — with the authors' own posted odds of producing a reportable result at ~45% and of matching the ancestor's (already modest, ~30%) quality at ~25%.

### Major concerns

1. **Issue** — The flagship "PASSED both axes" causal gate is reported at the same small, single-recipe scale that the document's own encoder comparison already proved produces illusory, non-generalising results, and there is no evidence it was re-tested under recipe variation.
   **Where** — "CURRENT STATE — 2026-08-24" ("POC-M causal gate PASSED both axes (concept mAP 1.000 p=0.0005; rank-invariance 0.917 p=0.0015...)"), sourced from "results/poc1c_organism_gate.json" with n=25 organisms per "## The claim." Contrast with "Encoder comparison results," which states the identically-scaled subspace_proj result (1.000 concept / 0.957 rank-invariance) was "a ceiling effect from saturating at 1.000" and collapsed to a tie (0.302 vs 0.325 vs 0.300) once recipe was allowed to vary.
   **Why it matters** — The entire justification for spending the workshop's 4-day, multi-GPU budget on reader training ("Building the reader... which is the contribution") rests on this gate having passed. The document has already demonstrated, once, that a perfect-looking score at this exact corpus size is not trustworthy until stress-tested against recipe variation. Nothing in the text shows the gate itself survived that same stress test — so the "go" decision may be built on the same artefact that was just discovered and discarded elsewhere in this document.
   **What would address it** — Re-run the POC-M concept-mAP / rank-invariance gate under the recipe-varied protocol used to refute the subspace-projector claim, and report both numbers side by side (as was done for the encoder), before treating the gate as a green light.

2. **Issue** — Corpus-size sufficiency and cross-modality/cross-architecture bridge viability are two independent unknowns, but the experimental design conflates them into a single expensive end-to-end run, and the justification for corpus size is imported from a population (matched-architecture text LoRAs) that the document itself says has no bearing on the population actually being tested (mismatched-architecture image LoRAs).
   **Where** — "H. Their scaling ablation" (flat val/mean_all_evals from 2,500 to 10,000 LoRAs, "That is good for us at ~10^3 organisms") versus "I. What does NOT transfer" point 1 ("Ours are not [native]... nothing in their results speaks to whether it works" for the klein-3072-wide → text-LLM bridge).
   **Why it matters** — If the reader underperforms at ~959 organisms, the design as described cannot distinguish "corpus was too small" from "the bridge carries no signal at all," because the ancestor's flat-scaling evidence was generated under a completely different distribution (matched architecture, self-referential residual stream, two orders of magnitude cheaper LoRAs). Applying that generalisation to a fundamentally different population is exactly the kind of external-validity transfer the authors should not make without evidence, and the document supplies none.
   **What would address it** — Isolate the two unknowns before the full run: e.g., a cheap same-modality/same-architecture replication at n≈1000 to confirm the flat-scaling property holds at this smaller N in principle, run separately from (or as a documented ablation within) the cross-modality bridge test, so a negative result at the end is attributable to one cause rather than either.

3. **Issue** — The control needed to rule out the reader "reading" corpus/template shortcuts instead of the weight-derived tokens is explicitly absent.
   **Where** — "D. Evaluation": "This detects the exact failure mode we are most exposed to at small n... **We have no such control and must add one.**"
   **Why it matters** — Without a cross-LoRA (AuditBench-style) control, any apparent success at generating correct descriptions is equally consistent with the model learning correlations between question templates/concept metadata and answers, rather than actually reading the injected weight tokens. At n≈959 with heavily templated supervision (adopted wholesale from the ancestor per §C), this shortcut is a live risk, and the paper cannot currently distinguish "the bridge carries signal" from "the model memorised the corpus's surface structure." This directly undermines any external-validity claim the workshop result would make.
   **What would address it** — Implement the cross-LoRA control before any result is drafted, and treat "AuditBench collapses to 0% while held-out keeps climbing" (the ancestor's own diagnostic signature) as a gating criterion for whether a result is reportable at all.

4. **Issue** — The featurizer comparison that drove (and then reversed) the paper's central claim was run on a very small sample (n=25–32 organisms) across roughly five candidate featurizers, with no stated correction for multiple comparisons and no confidence intervals reported for any of the point estimates.
   **Where** — "## The claim" (subspace_proj 1.000/0.957, our_svd 0.479, spectrum-only ablation 0.624, sign-invariant uᵢvᵢᵀ 0.53, u1+logreg 0.703, spectral 0.539) and "Encoder comparison results" (0.302 vs 0.325 vs 0.300 under recipe variation).
   **Why it matters** — Selecting a "winner" among several candidate features on n=25 without correction is precisely how the document arrived at the now-retracted subspace_proj claim. The retraction confirms the overfitting risk was real once — nothing shown in the text indicates the surviving numbers (u1 0.703, spectral 0.539, the POC-M gate) were derived any more robustly, so the same failure mode could be silently present in whatever is adopted next.
   **What would address it** — Report bootstrap confidence intervals over organisms for every comparison used to select a component, and correct for the number of featurizers/ablations examined, rather than reporting single point estimates as decisive.

5. **Issue** — The core novelty claim ("Nobody has done this for images") and the two quantitative baselines are attributed to sources that cannot be verified from the text as given.
   **Where** — "Africa et al. and the backdoor detector both emit a flag, not a description" (no year, venue, or full citation); "u₁+logreg (`2607.25750`)" and "spectral (`2602.15195`-style)" (bare numeric identifiers, no titles or authors).
   **Why it matters** — The claim that this is the first descriptive (non-flag) weight-space reader for image models is the paper's stated reason for existing as a workshop contribution. As written, a reader cannot check whether "Africa et al." indeed only emits a binary flag, whether other descriptive systems exist, or whether the cited IDs correspond to the described baselines' actual reported numbers.
   **What would address it** — Full citations (authors, venue/year, and what each system's output actually is) for all three references, with enough detail that the novelty claim and baseline numbers can be checked independently.

6. **Issue** — The corpus, by the authors' own account, includes backdoored adapters ("0/3 backdoors fire" is listed as an open bug), and the document contains no discussion of containment, disclosure, or release policy for backdoor-trigger organisms — despite stating this material will be reused verbatim in a later archival ICLR submission.
   **Where** — "Next actions" ("0/3 backdoors fire"); "The split, and why it does not cost the ICLR paper" (content is "reusable verbatim... without rewriting").
   **Why it matters** — Minting and studying backdoored LoRA adapters is a legitimate research activity, but training/distributing adapters designed to trigger hidden behaviour carries dual-use risk that a paper (even a workshop plan) should name explicitly — what happens to these organisms if the corpus or checkpoints are shared, and whether the backdoor triggers themselves are disclosed responsibly.
   **What would address it** — A short, explicit statement of how backdoor-organism weights/checkpoints will be handled (kept private, redacted triggers, restricted release, etc.) before the corpus is reused in the archival submission.

7. **Issue** — There is no pre-registered stopping rule or falsification criterion for the 4-day sprint, and the scope has already shifted once after a headline claim was refuted, which creates room for the bar to move again rather than for a negative result to be reported as such.
   **Where** — "The split, and why it does not cost the ICLR paper" (workshop scope changed from "the encoder result" to "the reader" on 2026-08-24, the same day the encoder headline was refuted); "Honest odds" (45% / 25%, stated with no reference class or method).
   **Why it matters** — Under an adversarial reading, a project that redefines its headline claim immediately after the original claim fails, and then assigns itself unexplained numeric odds of success, is at risk of doing so again if the reader also underperforms — e.g., by quietly redefining "reportable" downward. Nothing in the text commits the authors to a specific held-out metric/threshold that would count as a negative result worth reporting as negative.
   **What would address it** — State, in advance of running the reader, the specific metric and threshold that would constitute (a) a positive result, (b) an honest negative/inconclusive result, and (c) grounds to abandon the port for the workshop — and commit to reporting whichever occurs.

### Minor concerns

- Arithmetic mismatch: "150 concepts x 6 replicates" implies 900 organisms, but the corpus is elsewhere described as "959-organism"; the discrepancy is unexplained.
- The judge model (`anthropic/claude-sonnet-4.6`, inherited from the ancestor's config in §G) has no reported human-agreement or calibration check for the new image-concept judging task, which differs from the ancestor's text-behaviour judging task it was validated on.
- It's unclear whether known-broken concept categories (`low_poly_3d`, which "nulls 4/4," and the 0/3 firing backdoors) are included in or excluded from the POC-M gate's reported 1.000 concept-mAP — if included, they should lower the score; if excluded, that should be stated, since silently dropping the hardest cases would inflate the metric.
- The document mixes journal entries, a refuted draft abstract, and literal code docstrings in one file; a reader has to reconstruct which claims are current, retracted, or aspirational (e.g., "## The claim" section presents subspace_proj's 1.000/0.957 without the caveat given later in "Encoder comparison results" that this exact claim is refuted).
- "our_svd... 0.479 — below its own spectrum-only ablation (0.624)" and the sign-invariance ablation (0.53) are reported as single point values with no sample size, CI, or significance test attached.

### Verdict

**Major revision** — the document shows real methodological self-correction (catching and reporting its own refuted headline), but the claim it now rests on (a passed causal gate, justifying a full cross-modality reader build) has not been shown to survive the exact stress test that just invalidated a structurally identical earlier claim, and a key leakage control is admittedly missing.
